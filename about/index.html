College was supposed to be out of reach.

I was born in Quezon City and moved to Tacoma when I was 15. Money was tight, and college felt like something for other families. I spent all four years of high school in Navy JROTC, and for a while the plan was simple: graduate, then enlist.

I applied to college anyway. Twenty-five schools said yes, with close to $1M in scholarship offers. I chose Claflin University, double-majored in finance and computer science, and studied in London as a Frederick Douglass Global Fellow.

Internships at Oracle, Bank of America, KPMG, Deloitte and Nestlé got me into Google’s finance rotation program, and I was promoted within the first year. But on every team I hit the same wall. The answers I needed were stuck in data I couldn’t get to.

So I learned to build it. I moved into data engineering on Core Strategy & Operations, and a year later I was a Data Engineer III. My focus is organizational health and strategy: headcount numbers people trust, reports that land in inboxes, and AI agents leaders actually use.

The camera came along the whole way. It started in high school and turned into FMU Photography.

I didn’t have many mentors. Now I try to be one.

At Google I started Kapwa, the Filipino network’s mentorship program. It grew to 110+ mentees and 58 mentors, including its first cohort from the Philippines. Outside work, I help students back home apply to college abroad.

On a mostly non-technical team, I became the person people asked about AI. That turned into office hours, an onboarding plan, dashboard guides, and a technical advisor role for AI Builders Week, open to all ~11,000 people in Core.
