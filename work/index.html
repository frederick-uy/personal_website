Problem → approach → result

Headcount & planning

One headcount number everyone trusts

Problem: HR, recruiting and finance each tracked headcount in their own system, and the numbers rarely matched. Leaders planning for an ~11,000-person org had no single view to plan from.

Approach: I joined the three sources into one dataset and worked with program managers to agree on definitions across the full headcount lifecycle: open roles, hires without requisitions and campus hires. On top of it, I built portfolio metrics for 600+ programs.

Result

One source of truth behind workforce planning and business reviews, used by executives, chiefs of staff, finance and recruiting partners, and cost center owners.

Business reviews & reporting

Email as a reporting service

Problem: Monthly and quarterly business reviews and weekly leadership updates depended on manual data pulls, and leaders rarely had time to check dashboards.

Approach: I automated the reporting first in Apps Script. When it couldn’t reach the internal data we needed, I evaluated internal tools and rebuilt the reports as scheduled workflows that render HTML/CSS emails from live data.

Why email? A metric only matters if someone acts on it, so the reports go to people’s inboxes, not just to dashboards.

Result

30 automated reports, including a weekly director newsletter on headcount and recruiting. The business review flow later became an AI agent.

AI & insights

An AI agent for org-health questions

Problem: Chiefs of staff and their teams had a constant stream of questions about the org, and each one meant waiting on an analyst.

Approach: I built the team’s first chatbot (Python, NotebookLM) and grew it into a RAG-based agent over headcount data. It answers both quantitative and qualitative questions, like “What’s the ratio of program managers to engineers?”

Result

Used by the chief of staff team and all 11 pillars, so leaders could get answers directly instead of filing requests.
